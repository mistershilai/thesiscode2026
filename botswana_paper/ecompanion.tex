\section{Dualization: Proof of Proposition~4.1}
\label{ec:dualization}

We derive the tractable single-level reformulation of the affinely-adjustable robust problem, establishing Proposition~4.1 of the main text.
Throughout, the affine decision rule is $F_{(u,v),k}^{(t)}(\xi) = \bar F_{(u,v),k}^{(t)} + \alpha_{(u,v),k}^{(t)}\,\xi_{v,k}^{(t)}$, and the uncertainty set is the budgeted set $\mathcal{U}^{(k)}$.

\emph{Inventory balance.}
Substituting the affine rule into the inventory balance and collecting the terms in $\xi$, the uncertain component at node $n$ is
\begin{equation}
\Big(\textstyle\sum_{j:(j,n)\in A}\alpha_{(j,n),k}^{(t)} - 1\Big)\xi_{n,k}^{(t)}
- \sum_{r:(n,r)\in A}\alpha_{(n,r),k}^{(t)}\xi_{r,k}^{(t)}.
\end{equation}
Robust feasibility requires that its worst case over $\mathcal{U}^{(k)}$ not exceed the deterministic slack:
\begin{align}
&\max_{\xi\in\mathcal{U}^{(k)}}\Big[\Big(\textstyle\sum_{j:(j,n)\in A}\alpha_{(j,n),k}^{(t)} - 1\Big)\xi_{n,k}^{(t)}
- \sum_{r:(n,r)\in A}\alpha_{(n,r),k}^{(t)}\xi_{r,k}^{(t)}\Big] \notag\\
&\qquad \le\; I_{n,k}^{(t)}
+ \textstyle\sum_{j:(j,n)\in A}\bar F_{(j,n),k}^{(t)}
- \textstyle\sum_{r:(n,r)\in A}\bar F_{(n,r),k}^{(t)} \notag\\
&\qquad\quad + \mathbf{1}_{\{n=0\}}q_k^{(t)}
- \bar D_{n,k}^{(t)} + U_{n,k}^{(t)} - I_{n,k}^{(t+1)}.
\label{eq:ec-inv-robust}
\end{align}
Because $\mathcal{U}^{(k)}$ is symmetric, this requires realized end-of-period stock plus the planned unmet demand $U_{n,k}^{(t)}$ to cover the planned inventory $I_{n,k}^{(t+1)} \ge 0$ for every $\xi \in \mathcal{U}^{(k)}$.
The planned $I_{n,k}^{(t+1)}$ and $U_{n,k}^{(t)}$ are chosen before $\xi$ is observed.
The simulator computes realized inventory and unmet demand from the executed shipments.

\emph{Availability and capacity.}
The same substitution into the shipment-availability constraint of the main text and the arc-capacity constraint yields uncertain components
\begin{equation}
\textstyle\sum_{r:(n,r)\in A}\alpha_{(n,r),k}^{(t)}\xi_{r,k}^{(t)} - \Big(\sum_{j:(j,n)\in A}\alpha_{(j,n),k}^{(t)}\Big)\xi_{n,k}^{(t)}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\textstyle\sum_{k}\alpha_{(i,j),k}^{(t)}\xi_{j,k}^{(t)},
\end{equation}
respectively, each of which must satisfy an analogous worst-case-$\le$-slack condition.

Using the Bertsimas--Sim representation $\xi_{r,k}^{(t)} = \sigma_{r,k}^{(t)}(z_{r,k}^{+} - z_{r,k}^{-})$ with $0 \le z_{r,k}^{\pm}\le 1$ and $\sum_r (z_{r,k}^{+}+z_{r,k}^{-})\le\Gamma$, each inner maximization is a linear program in $(z^{+},z^{-})$.
Taking its linear-programming dual replaces the maximization by a minimization over nonnegative multipliers, and because the outer problem minimizes, the two combine into a single minimization.
For the inventory constraint we introduce a budget multiplier $\theta_{n,k}^{(t)}$ and per-node deviation multipliers $\pi_{n,k,r}^{\pm(t)}$, giving the finite system
\begin{align}
\Gamma\,\theta_{n,k}^{(t)} + \textstyle\sum_{r\in R(n)}\big(\pi_{n,k,r}^{+(t)}+\pi_{n,k,r}^{-(t)}\big)
\;\le\;& I_{n,k}^{(t)}
+ \textstyle\sum_{j:(j,n)\in A}\bar F_{(j,n),k}^{(t)}
- \sum_{r:(n,r)\in A}\bar F_{(n,r),k}^{(t)} \notag\\
& + \mathbf{1}_{\{n=0\}}q_k^{(t)} - \bar D_{n,k}^{(t)} + U_{n,k}^{(t)} - I_{n,k}^{(t+1)}, \\
\theta_{n,k}^{(t)} + \pi_{n,k,n}^{+(t)} \;\ge\;& \Big(\textstyle\sum_{j:(j,n)\in A}\alpha_{(j,n),k}^{(t)}-1\Big)\sigma_{n,k}^{(t)}, \\
\theta_{n,k}^{(t)} + \pi_{n,k,n}^{-(t)} \;\ge\;& -\Big(\textstyle\sum_{j:(j,n)\in A}\alpha_{(j,n),k}^{(t)}-1\Big)\sigma_{n,k}^{(t)},
\end{align}
together with the analogous inequalities for each $r$ with $(n,r)\in A$, all multipliers nonnegative.
Here $R(n) = \{n\}\cup\{r : (n,r)\in A\}$: multipliers for other nodes are identically zero and omitted.
The availability and arc-capacity constraints dualize identically, with multipliers $(\tilde\theta,\tilde\pi^{\pm})$ and $(\eta,\rho^{\pm})$ respectively, and the resulting inequalities have the same structure and are omitted.

Each robust constraint has been replaced by finitely many linear inequalities in the original variables $(\bar F,\alpha,I,U,q,\lambda,y)$ and the nonnegative multipliers $(\theta,\pi^{\pm},\tilde\theta,\tilde\pi^{\pm},\eta,\rho^{\pm})$.
The inventory and availability constraints introduce one budget multiplier per node--class and one deviation multiplier pair per element of $R(n)$, and the capacity constraints introduce one multiplier pair per arc--class, so the reformulation adds $O(|N|K + |A|K)$ variables and constraints.
The adjustable robust allocation problem is therefore an exact single-level mixed-integer linear program of size linear in $|N|$, $|A|$, and $K$, which proves Proposition~4.1. \hfill$\blacksquare$

% ======================= EC.2 EXACTNESS OF ADRs =======================
\section{Exactness of Affine Decision Rules}
\label{ec:exactness}

This section proves Theorems~4.1, 4.2, and~4.3 of the main text.
We suppress the indices $(t)$ and $k$ throughout, since the mechanism is identical for each period and class, and we work with CMS supply $S$ directly: both theorems spend the entire budget, so $S=B/c^{\text{proc}}$ recovers the main text's statement once $B$ is fixed.
Demand at node $n$ is $D_n = \bar D_n + \xi_n$, with $\bar D_n \ge 0$ and $\xi_n$ in the budgeted set $\mathcal{U} = \{ \xi\in\reals^{|N|} : |\xi_n|\le\sigma_n\ \forall n,\ \sum_{n\in N}|\xi_n|/\sigma_n\le\Gamma \}$, and the affine decision rule on arc $(u,v)$ is $F_{(u,v)}(\xi_v) = \bar F_{(u,v)} + \alpha_{(u,v)}\xi_v$, the main text's rule with the suppressed indices restored.

\subsection{Single-Echelon Network}
\label{ec:exactness-single}

Suppose CMS has $S\ge0$ units available and ships directly to facilities $n\in N$.
A feasible shipment rule satisfies $F_n(\xi_n)\ge0$ and $\sum_{n\in N}F_n(\xi_n)\le S$ for every $\xi\in\mathcal{U}$.
Unmet demand at facility $n$ is $U_n(\xi) = [\bar D_n+\xi_n-F_n(\xi_n)]^+$, and the robust service objective is $W(F) = \max_{\xi\in\mathcal{U}}\sum_{n\in N}U_n(\xi)$.
Let $M := \max_{\xi\in\mathcal{U}}\sum_{n\in N}(\bar D_n+\xi_n)$ denote the worst-case total demand.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~4.1]
Assume $\bar D_n\ge\sigma_n$ for every $n\in N$.
For any feasible shipment rule and any $\xi\in\mathcal{U}$, subadditivity of $t\mapsto t^+$ gives
\begin{equation}
\textstyle\sum_n U_n(\xi) \;\ge\; \big[\sum_n(\bar D_n+\xi_n-F_n(\xi_n))\big]^+
\;\ge\; \big[\sum_n(\bar D_n+\xi_n) - S\big]^+,
\end{equation}
where the second inequality uses $\sum_n F_n(\xi_n)\le S$.
Taking the maximum over $\xi$ gives $W(F)\ge(M-S)^+$ for every feasible rule, affine or not, so no procurement level $q$ attains a value below $(M-q)^+$ at $S=q$.
Since $q\mapsto(M-q)^+$ is nonincreasing and feasibility restricts $q$ only through $c^{\text{proc}}q\le B$, spending the entire budget ($S=B/c^{\text{proc}}$) is optimal.

Now consider $F_n(\xi_n) = \lambda(\bar D_n+\xi_n)$ with $\lambda := \min\{1, S/M\}$ (and $\lambda=1$ if $M=0$).
Because $\bar D_n\ge\sigma_n$, realized demand is nonnegative throughout $\mathcal{U}$, so $F_n(\xi_n)\ge0$.
Also $\sum_n F_n(\xi_n) = \lambda\sum_n(\bar D_n+\xi_n)\le\lambda M\le S$, so the rule is feasible.
Since $0\le\lambda\le1$ and $\bar D_n+\xi_n\ge0$, unmet demand is $U_n(\xi) = (1-\lambda)(\bar D_n+\xi_n)$ exactly, with the truncation at zero never active.
Summing and taking the maximum over $\xi$ gives $W(F) = (1-\lambda)M = (M-S)^+$, matching the lower bound and proving the theorem at $S=B/c^{\text{proc}}$.
\end{proof}

Under the main text's demand calibration $\sigma_n=\sqrt{\bar D_n+\bar D_n^2/\kappa}$, the hypothesis $\bar D_n\ge\sigma_n$ holds once $\bar D_n\ge\kappa/(\kappa-1)$, about $1.1$ at $\kappa=10$.

\subsection{Multi-Echelon Tree Network}
\label{ec:exactness-tree}

Now the distribution network is a rooted tree with CMS at node $0$.
Non-CMS node $n$ has upstream node $p(n)$ and downstream nodes $C(n)$, and we write $F_n(\xi_n) := F_{(p(n),n)}(\xi_n)$.
A node must have enough inflow to meet its own demand and every shipment sent downstream,
\begin{equation}
F_n(\xi_n) \;\ge\; \bar D_n+\xi_n+\textstyle\sum_{c\in C(n)}F_c(\xi_c),
\label{eq:ec-flow}
\end{equation}
and when inflow falls short, unmet demand at $n$ is
\begin{equation}
U_n(\xi) := \Big[\bar D_n+\xi_n+\textstyle\sum_{c\in C(n)}F_c(\xi_c)-F_n(\xi_n)\Big]^+,
\label{eq:ec-unmet-tree}
\end{equation}
recovering $U_n(\xi)=[\bar D_n+\xi_n-F_n(\xi_n)]^+$ at a leaf.
We characterize the minimum CMS supply guaranteeing $U_n(\xi)=0$ everywhere.

Let $\Gamma=k$ be a positive integer, and for nonnegative numbers $g_c$ define the fractional-knapsack value
\begin{equation}
H_n(r) := \max\Big\{ \textstyle\sum_{c\in C(n)}g_cz_c \ :\ 0\le z_c\le1,\ \sum_{c\in C(n)}z_c\le r \Big\},
\label{eq:ec-H}
\end{equation}
the sum of the $k$ largest values among $\{g_c:c\in C(n)\}$ at $r=k$ (or all of them if $|C(n)|<k$).
Build, from the lowest tier upward, the nominal shipment $A_n$ into $n$ and the largest increase $g_n$ that shipment can undergo as $\xi_n$ rises from $0$ to $\sigma_n$: at a leaf,
\begin{equation}
A_n = \bar D_n, \qquad g_n=\sigma_n,
\label{eq:ec-leaf}
\end{equation}
and at an internal node, with $\rho_n$ the $k$-th largest value among $\{g_c:c\in C(n)\}$ (or $0$ if $|C(n)|<k$),
\begin{equation}
A_n = \bar D_n + \textstyle\sum_{c\in C(n)}A_c + H_n(k), \qquad
g_n = (\sigma_n-\rho_n)^+, \qquad \alpha_n = g_n/\sigma_n,
\label{eq:ec-A}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_n := 0$ when $\sigma_n = 0$.
A node with $\sigma_n = 0$, such as a warehouse with no demand of its own, has $\xi_n = 0$ throughout $\mathcal U$ and $g_n = 0$.
Its inflow is therefore static, and adaptivity enters only through nodes with demand.

The affine decision rules
\begin{equation}
F_n(\xi_n) = A_n+\alpha_n\xi_n,
\label{eq:ec-adr-tree}
\end{equation}
with $A_n$ and $\alpha_n$ given by Equations~\ref{eq:ec-leaf}--\ref{eq:ec-A}, are the ones Theorem~4.2 refers to, attaining the minimum required supply $S_{\rm zero} = \sum_{c\in C(0)}A_c+H_0(k)$ (with $H_0$ built from the root-child values $g_c$) and, equivalently, the minimum budget $B_{\rm zero}=c^{\text{proc}}S_{\rm zero}$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~4.2]
Assume $\bar D_n\ge\sigma_n$ for every $n\ne0$, and let $\Gamma=k\in\{1,2,\dots\}$.
Working from the lowest tier upward, we show by induction that, with $\underline F_n(x) := A_n+x-(\rho_n/\sigma_n)|x|$ and $\rho_n=0$ at a leaf, (i) every policy guaranteeing zero unmet demand satisfies $F_n(x)\ge\underline F_n(x)$ for $x\in[-\sigma_n,\sigma_n]$, and (ii) the rule of Equation~\ref{eq:ec-adr-tree} attains this bound.
At a leaf, $\underline F_n(x)=\bar D_n+x$ and the rule $F_n(x)=\bar D_n+x$ attains it, so both hold at the lowest tier.

For an internal node $n$ whose children satisfy (i)-(ii), split $\underline F_c$ by the sign of its argument: $\underline F_c(y)=A_c+(1+\rho_c/\sigma_c)y$ for $y\le0$ and $A_c+(1-\rho_c/\sigma_c)y$ for $y\ge0$, so a negative deviation never raises $\underline F_c$ above $A_c$ and a positive one raises it by at most $g_c=(\sigma_c-\rho_c)^+$, at $y=\sigma_c$.
Fix $\xi_n=x$.
Since the remaining tree coordinates can be set to zero, admissible child deviations satisfy $\sum_{c}|\xi_c|/\sigma_c\le k-|x|/\sigma_n$, and statement (i) at each child together with Equation~\ref{eq:ec-flow} gives
\begin{equation}
F_n(x) \;\ge\; \bar D_n+x+\max_{\substack{|\xi_c|\le\sigma_c\\ \sum_c|\xi_c|/\sigma_c\le k-|x|/\sigma_n}}\ \textstyle\sum_{c\in C(n)}\underline F_c(\xi_c)
\;=\; \bar D_n+x+\textstyle\sum_{c\in C(n)}A_c+H_n\big(k-\tfrac{|x|}{\sigma_n}\big),
\label{eq:ec-necessary-max}
\end{equation}
the equality because negative child deviations never increase the maximum and each child contributes at most $g_c$, matching the knapsack objective of Equation~\ref{eq:ec-H}.
Since $k$ is an integer and $|x|/\sigma_n\in[0,1]$, $H_n$ is linear on $[k-1,k]$ with slope $\rho_n$, so $H_n(k-|x|/\sigma_n)=H_n(k)-\rho_n|x|/\sigma_n$.
Combined with the definition of $A_n$, Equation~\ref{eq:ec-necessary-max} gives $F_n(x)\ge\underline F_n(x)$, proving (i) at $n$.

For (ii), a direct check of $A_n+\alpha_nx$ against $\underline F_n(x)$ in each of $x\ge0$ and $x\le0$ (using $\alpha_n=(\sigma_n-\rho_n)^+/\sigma_n\le1\le1+\rho_n/\sigma_n$) shows the rule always meets the bound, with equality whenever $\rho_n\le\sigma_n$ and $x\ge0$.
Its largest increase above $A_n$ is exactly $\alpha_n\sigma_n=g_n$, so $(A_n,g_n)$ satisfies the same recursive definition one level up, closing the induction.

At CMS, statement (i) at each root child gives $S\ge\sum_{c\in C(0)}A_c+H_0(k)=S_{\rm zero}$ as necessary, and statement (ii) shows the constructed rules meet it, so $S_{\rm zero}$ is also sufficient, proving the theorem at $S=B/c^{\text{proc}}=S_{\rm zero}$, i.e.\ $B=B_{\rm zero}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Value of Adaptivity}
\label{ec:gap}

We keep the notation of \Sec\ref{ec:exactness-tree} with $\Gamma = k$, set $H_n(k) := 0$ at a leaf, and work with supply, so that $\Delta = c^{\text{proc}}(S_{\rm static} - S_{\rm zero})$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~4.3]
\emph{Static budget.}
A static rule ships a constant $F_n$ into each node.
Since $k \ge 1$, the deviation with $\xi_n = \sigma_n$ and every other coordinate zero lies in $\mathcal U$, and at it Equation~\ref{eq:ec-flow} requires $F_n \ge \bar D_n + \sigma_n + \sum_{c \in C(n)} F_c$.
Induction from the leaves gives $S \ge \sum_{n \neq 0} (\bar D_n + \sigma_n)$, and shipping into each node the sum of $\bar D_m + \sigma_m$ over its subtree meets Equation~\ref{eq:ec-flow} for every $\xi \in \mathcal U$.
So $S_{\rm static} = \sum_{n \neq 0} (\bar D_n + \sigma_n)$ for every integer $k \ge 1$.

\emph{Lower bound.}
Unrolling Equation~\ref{eq:ec-A} gives $S_{\rm zero} = \sum_{n \neq 0} \bar D_n + \sum_{n \in N} H_n(k)$, and every non-CMS node is the child of exactly one node, so
\begin{equation}
S_{\rm static} - S_{\rm zero} \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{n \in N} \big( \sum_{c \in C(n)} \sigma_c - H_n(k) \big).
\label{eq:ec-gap-sum}
\end{equation}
The sum of the $k$ largest entries of a vector is nondecreasing in each entry and $g_c \le \sigma_c$, so $H_n(k) \le \sum_{c \in C_\Gamma(n)} \sigma_c$, and each term of Equation~\ref{eq:ec-gap-sum} is at least $\sum_{c \in C(n) \setminus C_\Gamma(n)} \sigma_c$.

\emph{Monotonicity in $\Gamma$.}
The budgeted set for $k$ is contained in the set for $k+1$, so the destination-dependent rule attaining $S_{\rm zero}$ at $k+1$ also guarantees zero unmet demand at $k$.
By the minimality in Theorem~4.2, $S_{\rm zero}$ is nondecreasing in $k$, and $S_{\rm static}$ does not depend on $k$.

\emph{Monotonicity in each $\sigma_j$.}
Let $\sigma'$ agree with $\sigma$ except $\sigma'_j = \sigma_j + \varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon > 0$, both satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem~4.2.
The static supply rises by exactly $\varepsilon$, so it suffices that $S_{\rm zero}$ rises by at most $\varepsilon$.
Let $F$ attain $S_{\rm zero}$ under $\sigma$, and map each $\xi'$ in the set for $\sigma'$ to $\xi$ in the set for $\sigma$ by $\xi_j = \xi'_j \sigma_j/\sigma'_j$, other coordinates unchanged, which preserves the budget term and gives $|\xi'_j - \xi_j| \le \varepsilon$.
Set $F'_j(\xi'_j) = F_j(\xi_j) + \xi'_j - \xi_j$, which is at least $\bar D_j + \xi'_j + \sum_{c \in C(j)} F_c \ge 0$, add $\varepsilon$ to the shipment into every other ancestor of $j$ and to the CMS supply, and leave all other shipments unchanged.
This rule is destination-dependent, and at each ancestor the shipment rises by $\varepsilon$ while the child on the path to $j$ receives at most $\varepsilon$ more, so Equation~\ref{eq:ec-flow} holds everywhere with supply $S_{\rm zero} + \varepsilon$.
By the minimality in Theorem~4.2, $S_{\rm static} - S_{\rm zero}$ does not decrease.
\end{proof}

Theorems~4.1--4.3 charge only procurement against the budget.
The full model also includes transport and holding cost, multiple periods and classes, inventory carryover, capacities, route activation, and storage constraints.

% ======================= EC.4 EPIDEMIC MODEL =======================
\section{The Epidemic Coupling}
\label{ec:seir}

This section gives the epidemic-driven extension summarized in Section~6.5 of the main text, and the two-strain model behind it.
The hospital population is partitioned into susceptible $S$, exposed and infectious for the drug-susceptible strain $E_s,I_s$, exposed and infectious for the drug-resistant strain $E_r,I_r$, and recovered $R$, with $N=S+E_s+I_s+E_r+I_r+R$.
Forces of infection are $\lambda_s = \beta_s I_s/N$ and $\lambda_r = \beta_r I_r/N$, with $\beta_r = 0.8\,\beta_s$ a fitness cost.
The dynamics are
\begin{align}
\dot S &= \mu N - (\lambda_s+\lambda_r)S - \mu S, &
\dot E_s &= \lambda_s S - (\sigma_s+\mu)E_s, \notag\\
\dot I_s &= \sigma_s E_s - (\tau_s+\gamma_s+\mu)I_s, &
\dot E_r &= \lambda_r S + \rho\,\tau_s I_s - (\sigma_r+\mu)E_r, \notag\\
\dot I_r &= \sigma_r E_r - (\tau_r+\gamma_r+\mu)I_r, &
\dot R &= \gamma_s I_s + \gamma_r I_r + (1-\rho)\tau_s I_s + \tau_r I_r - \mu R.
\end{align}
The amplification probability $\rho$ is the fraction of treated susceptible infections that convert to resistant rather than recovering.
The coupling to the supply chain enters through the treatment rates,
\begin{equation}
\tau_s = \tau_s^{0}\min\!\Big(\tfrac{a_1}{a_1^{\star}},1\Big), \qquad
\tau_r = \tau_r^{0}\min\!\Big(\tfrac{a_2}{a_2^{\star}},1\Big),
\end{equation}
where $a_1,a_2$ are the realized supply rates of cephalosporins and carbapenems and $a_1^{\star},a_2^{\star}$ their nominal levels from the MDE, so a stockout depresses treatment and, through the $\rho\tau_s I_s$ term, feeds resistance.

The system runs in closed loop with the allocation model.
Each period sets demand from the current infectious populations, $\bar D^{(t)}_{\text{ceph}} = \tau_s^{0} I_s^{(t)}\Delta t$ and $\bar D^{(t)}_{\text{carb}} = \tau_r^{0} I_r^{(t)}\Delta t$, solves the allocation problem for realized supply $a_1^{(t)}, a_2^{(t)}$, and advances the compartments over $\Delta t$ at that supply.
Table~\ref{tab:seir-params} lists the parameters and their sources.
Rates are per month, and $\beta_s$ is scaled by the rainy-season multiplier from November to March.
Hospital-specific values come from Princess Marina Hospital, the baseline susceptible treatment rate from Botswana coverage data, and several biological rates from a general two-strain model \citep{kd20}, so the calibration is illustrative.

\paragraph{Closed-loop results.}
The evaluation so far treats demand as statistically uncertain but exogenous.
We make demand epidemiologically driven by coupling the allocation model to a two-strain compartmental model at Princess Marina Hospital.
The two strains are drug-susceptible (DS), treated with first-line cephalosporins, and drug-resistant (DR), treated with second-line carbapenems.
Treatment intensity is tied to realized supply: the DS and DR treatment rates scale with the delivered fractions of cephalosporins and carbapenems, saturating at their nominal levels.
A stockout therefore suppresses treatment, prolonging infectiousness and feeding a resistance-amplification pathway in which imperfectly treated DS infections convert to DR.
The coupling runs closed-loop, one period at a time: we solve the allocation, observe the cephalosporin and carbapenem that reached the hospital, advance the compartmental system under that supply, and draw the next period's demand from the updated infectious population.
Each policy therefore faces the demand its own stockouts create.
The allocation models do not anticipate this feedback, which enters through the environment rather than the objective.
We present this as a proof-of-concept coupling rather than a validated epidemiological model.
Several rates in Table~\ref{tab:seir-params} come from a general two-strain model and are slow for an inpatient setting, so the experiment illustrates the feedback mechanism rather than hospital dynamics.

Over two years the ordering is unchanged: ARO-ADR leaves 2.1\% of demand unmet on average, against 3.7\% for static robust and 12.5\% for deterministic (Table~\ref{tab:epi}).
In its worst period its shortfall is 21.8\%, against 39.4\% for deterministic.
Because treatment depends on what arrives, ARO-ADR meets 98.8\% of the hospital's cephalosporin requirement against 91.4\% under deterministic planning, and ends the horizon with fewer infectious patients of both strains.
The effect on resistance is more subtle.
At two years ARO-ADR carries a lower \emph{absolute} resistant burden but a slightly higher resistant \emph{fraction}, because clearing susceptible infections faster shrinks the denominator.
The fraction stays slightly higher over a 50-year horizon, while the absolute burden stays lower for two decades before the paths meet, as the long-horizon extension below shows.
Supply reliability therefore reduces resistant infections over a planning horizon without changing the long-run resistant share, and the calibration is illustrative, so we do not read the magnitudes as forecasts.

\paragraph{Two-year resistance dynamics.}
Over the two-year horizon of the main-text extension, the deterministic policy's chronic cephalosporin shortages slow clearance of the susceptible strain and sustain the amplification pathway.
The robust policies clear both strains faster (Figure~\ref{fig:resistance}).
Static robust and ARO-ADR are nearly indistinguishable here ($\rho = 0.07$).

\paragraph{Long-horizon behavior.}
Extending the integration to 50 years under each policy's steady coverage, the resistant \emph{fraction} $I_r/(I_s+I_r)$ stays marginally higher under ARO-ADR at every horizon, $43.6\%$ against $42.7\%$ at two years and $68.6\%$ against $68.1\%$ at 50 years, because better coverage clears the susceptible strain faster.
The absolute resistant burden $I_r$ is lower under ARO-ADR for the first two decades, $3.97$ against $4.95$ at 20 years, and the two paths meet by 50 years ($3.41$ against $3.38$).
Better supply therefore reduces resistant infections over the horizon a planner acts on, without changing the long-run resistant share.


\paragraph{Sensitivity to the amplification rate.}
Repeating the extension for $\rho$ from $0.01$ to $0.30$, the resistant fraction under ARO-ADR stays above the deterministic path throughout the 50 years for every $\rho$, so the comparison of fractions does not depend on the amplification rate.

\paragraph{Uncertainty budget.}
Figure~\ref{fig:gamma} varies the budget $\Gamma$ across dispersion levels.
Static robust is flat in $\Gamma$, since for $\Gamma \ge 1$ its protection at each node already covers that node's full deviation.
ARO-ADR improves as $\Gamma$ grows, so its advantage over static robust widens, from $3.7$ to $4.8$ percentage points at $\kappa = 2$ and from $1.2$ to $2.8$ at $\kappa = 10$ between $\Gamma = 1$ and $\Gamma = 50$.
The advantage is largest at high variability at every $\Gamma$.

\paragraph{Seasonal (non-stationary) demand.}
Mean demand follows $s(t)\,\bar D_{n,k}$, with the two-harmonic multiplier $s(t) = 1 + A_1 \sin(2\pi t/P + \pi/2) + A_2 \sin(4\pi t/P - \pi/4)$ over a cycle of $P = 26$ fortnightly periods.
The amplitudes $A_1 = 0.7249$ and $A_2 = -0.5585$ are fitted so that $s(1) = 1.87$ and $s(13) = 0.67$, the $+87\%$ peak and $-33\%$ trough in tuberculosis diagnoses that \citet{ballif2018} report for the Newlands site in Zimbabwe.
Pinning two points does not pin the range: over a cycle $s(t)$ runs from $0.08$ to $2.24$, a stronger swing than the reported one, so the experiment stresses the policies harder than the cited seasonality would.
The experiment covers aminoglycosides, quinolones and lincosamides, applying the tuberculosis pattern to all three as a stylized seasonal profile, sets $\kappa = 50$ to isolate the seasonal signal from noise, and runs two cycles, with each period's budget set from that period's seasonal forecast.
Given only stationary forecasts, mean unmet demand peaks at $55\%$ under deterministic planning, $51\%$ under ARO-ADR, and $40\%$ under static robust.
Here the robust policies trade places, and over the whole horizon static robust leaves $8.5\%$ of demand unmet against $13.2\%$ for ARO-ADR and $15.5\%$ for deterministic.
The affine rule hedges around a mean that is wrong for most of the cycle, while the static policy's larger buffer absorbs more of the error.
When the mean itself is wrong, forecasting it matters more than the choice of hedge.
Given the seasonally-adjusted means, ARO-ADR attains a mean unmet rate of $0.71\%$, against $2.41\%$ for static robust and $9.51\%$ for deterministic (Figure~\ref{fig:seasonal}).

\paragraph{Persistent demand.}
Independent draws reset demand every period, while an outbreak raises it for several periods in a row.
We therefore also drive the Gaborone instance with a negative-binomial draw whose intensity is $\bar D_{n,k}$ times a lognormal factor with mean one, built from a common and a class-specific component with total log-volatility $0.12$ per period.
With $\theta = 0$ the factor is a martingale, so shocks persist indefinitely, and with $\theta = 0.1$ its logarithm reverts to zero with a half-life of about seven periods.
The policies keep the nominal forecast, and the robust policies run twice: with the independent-draw deviation bound $\sigma = (\bar D + \bar D^2/\kappa)^{1/2}$ of the main text, and with a bound recalibrated to the persistent process's marginal variance, averaged over the horizon.
Over $T = 100$ periods, mean unmet demand is

The ordering holds in every case, and ARO-ADR remains best.
Service degrades with persistence because every policy plans on a fixed forecast while demand drifts away from it, and recalibrating the deviation bound recovers about half of that loss under the martingale.

\paragraph{Minimum stock levels.}
CMS writes stock policy as a minimum cover: an elastic floor requiring every facility to end each period holding at least $\beta$ periods of its nominal demand, swept across $\beta$ (Figure~\ref{fig:floor}).
Raising $\beta$ from $0$ to $2$ multiplies the deterministic policy's held inventory by $14.3$ and its total cost by $1.33$.
Its unmet demand falls by $2.2$ percentage points, against $11.5$ points from switching to ARO-ADR with no floor, at $88\%$ of that cost.
